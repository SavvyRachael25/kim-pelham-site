Have you ever thought about doing a for sale by owner? I have a service for that. This month it turned into a whole story.

These folks started with the for sale by owner.

It started with his father. He was going into assisted living and the family wanted to sell his house themselves. They were not confident about handling the paperwork and the back side of things, so I managed the paperwork for them for 1%. That is what the 1% is. You do the selling, I will do your paperwork.

Then they went to sell their own house in Bothell the same way, and she discovered they could not put it on Zillow. Several years ago they could. Now that Zillow is a member of our MLS, they will not take for sale by owners, in the state of Washington only. Other states, you still can. It is kind of an interesting fact and almost nobody knows it until they run into it.

So we put it on as a limited service listing. They did an open house on their own. They had a couple of showings. And she was getting really, really stressed, because they have a deadline and they need to sell. The market is different now. It is not like it was three years ago. It is hard right now, and they really needed professional advice.
 

and then we started over,

Same house, relaunched Thursday.

We cancelled the limited service listing and took it on properly. It is a partial stage, not a deep one, because they have already moved half their stuff to Idaho and they did not want to buy more. But it looks really good.

"I love the decor. It's crazy how it changes the whole dynamics of the house."

That is what she said when she walked in and saw it. Your house is set up for living. It is not set up for selling. Those are two completely different strategies, in how you decorate and how you arrange things.

Professional photos. A 3D tour on the way. It went live on Thursday.

One more change, and it matters more than people think. The first time around they were not offering to pay the buyer's agent. Now they are. A buyer cannot finance that commission, so it comes out of their pocket on top of the down payment, and right now, with rates where they are, that is the thing that makes them go look at the house down the street instead. 17104 24th Ave SE in Bothell, $715,000. the numbers,

Rates crossed 7%.

Freddie Mac's 30-year hit 7.03% on Thursday. It was 6.95% the week before. That is the first time it has been over 7% since January 2025, and the fifth week in a row it has gone up.

What does that mean in real money? On a $724,500 house, which is the county median, with 20% down, the payment is now about $3,868 a month. Two weeks ago it was $3,763. That is $105 more a month, or about $1,250 a year, for the same house.

It is also why I just spent a paragraph on who pays the buyer's agent. When money is this tight, every dollar a buyer has to find out of pocket decides which house they go see.
