For a friend of mine, it started in the Spring of 2024 when the elderly father moved into assisted living, and the family wanted to sell his house themselves. They put a sign in the yard and quickly found a buyer. They were not confident about handling the contracts, so I managed the paperwork, inspections, escrow, title and closing details for them for 1%.

In the Fall of 2026, they wanted to sell their own house in Bothell the same way, and we had the same arrangement - they do the showings, find the buyer and negotiate the price - I do the contracts and manage the closing for 1%. My friend discovered they could not list the home on Zillow, where several years ago they could. Zillow is a member of the NWMLS, and newer MLS rules preclude Zillow from showing For Sale By Owner (FSBO) homes on its site in Washington State. Interestingly, this is only in Washington - FSBOs can be shown on Zillow in other states.

I moved them to a Limited Service Listing, which added the home to the NWMLS. They held an open house on their own and had a couple of showings but activity dropped off. She was getting really stressed because they have a deadline for when they need to sell. The market has completely changed since March 2024: the number of active homes for sale in Snohomish County was 424 then, and it is 3,071 today. The market today requires expert advice and strategy to get your home sold.

and then we started over,

Same house - relaunched Thursday.

We cancelled the limited service listing and went to work, applying the Pelham Listing Principles. First was a staging consultation, which resulted in my doing a partial stage. They have done so much work and the home is in excellent condition. I edited the existing items in the home and brought in some art, soft goods and decor items to shift the home's energy.

The client was pleasantly surprised at the changes! I explained that your house is set up for living, it is not set up for selling. Those are two completely different strategies for decorating and arranging things.

Professional photos were completed and 3D tour photography was scheduled. Feature cards and signs were installed. The listing went live on Thursday.

One more change, and it matters more than people think. The first time around they were not offering to pay the buyer's agent commission. I recommended that they offer a 2.5% commission to the buyer's agent. If the seller doesn't pay the commission, it cannot be added to the buyer's mortgage, so it comes out of their pocket on top of the down payment and closing costs. Right now, with interest rates topping 7%, it is very difficult for buyers to afford a home. Not offering to pay their agent's commission can make buyers look at the house down the street instead.

the numbers,

Rates crossed 7%.

Freddie Mac's 30-year hit 7.03% on Thursday. It was 6.95% the week before. That is the first time it has been over 7% since January 2025, and the fifth week in a row it has gone up.

What does that mean in real money? On a $724,500 house, which is the county median, with 20% down, the payment is now about $3,868 a month. Two weeks ago it was $3,763. That is $105 more a month, or about $1,250 a year, for the same house. It's not a huge dollar difference per month, but it is a mental hurdle for buyers when interest rates top 7%.

It is also why I just spent a paragraph on who pays the buyer's agent. When money is this tight, every dollar a buyer has to find out of pocket decides which house they go see.
