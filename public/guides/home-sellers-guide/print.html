Chapter 01 · Pricing

The price is the whole game.

Almost every listing problem traces back to a number set in the wrong week. Pricing is not where you start the negotiation. It is the negotiation.

Here is what I see most often. A seller anchors to what a neighbor got six months ago, an agent agrees to it because they want the listing, and the home goes live priced for a market that does not exist anymore. By week three the listing is stale, buyers assume something is wrong with it, and the eventual sale comes in under what an honest number would have produced on day one.

Most of that is preventable. The prep work up front is the difference. A thoughtful pricing conversation starts with real comps (closed in the last 45 days, in the same submarket, accounting for view, lot, and condition) and an in-person walk-through of the home before any number ends up on paper. The goal is to land on what the house actually appraises against today, not what it could have last spring.

Chapter 02 · Timeline

The 90 days before we list.

A good listing is not a sprint at the end. It is a sequence. Here is what it looks like when it is run well.

T–903 months out

Walk-through and honest assessment

I come over, we go room by room. I tell you what to fix, what to leave alone, what to declutter, and what to spend money on. I also pull early comps so we can talk about a price band, not a number yet.

T–602 months out

Pre-listing inspection & sewer scope

We get ahead of every surprise a buyer's inspector would find. Roof, foundation, electrical, plumbing, sewer line. Fixing what is fixable now beats negotiating it on the back end.

T–301 month out

Repairs, paint, decluttering

Contractors lined up. Walls get a fresh neutral coat where it matters. We clear half of what is on every shelf. Garage gets emptied of anything that does not need to be in the photos.

T–142 weeks out

Staging weekend & photography

I stage with my own pieces (sofas, art, lamps, rugs) over a weekend. Photographer comes the following Tuesday morning, golden hour. Drone shots if the lot calls for it. Twilights if the view does.

T–71 week out

MLS prep, disclosures, pricing call

We finalize the price with the most recent week of comps. I write the listing copy. You sign disclosures. We coordinate the live date for a Thursday or Friday to maximize weekend traffic.

Day 0live on MLS

Listing goes live · open house weekend

Hits MLS Thursday morning. Open house Saturday and Sunday, twelve to three. I sit them both. By Sunday evening we know if we are reviewing offers Monday.

Chapter 03 · Diligence

The unsexy stuff that decides every sale.

Buyers fall in love with the kitchen. They walk away because of the roof. We get every one of these in front of you before they get in front of a buyer's inspector.

Pre-listing inspection

  • Roof age, condition, and remaining life
  • Attic ventilation and insulation
  • Crawlspace, foundation, drainage
  • Electrical panel and amperage
  • Plumbing supply lines and water heater age
  • Sewer scope to the city main
  • Furnace, ducts, and filter condition
  • Windows, seals, and visible flashing

Documents I will gather for you

  • Form 17 seller disclosure (every line walked through)
  • Permit history for any work done
  • HOA documents, CC&Rs, dues, reserves
  • Most recent property tax statement
  • Survey or plat map if available
  • Warranties on roof, HVAC, appliances
  • Septic records, if applicable
  • Mineral, water, and easement disclosures

Repairs I usually recommend

  • Replace any failed window seals (cheap, obvious to buyers)
  • Service the furnace and clean ducts
  • Caulk and re-grout bathrooms thoroughly
  • Fix every running toilet and slow drain
  • Touch up paint at door frames and baseboards
  • Power wash siding, walkways, deck
  • Replace any burnt-out bulbs (every one)
  • Address pet smells before staging, not after

Repairs I will tell you to skip

  • Full kitchen or bath remodels (you won't recoup)
  • Carpet replacement if the existing is clean
  • New appliances unless the current ones are broken
  • Driveway resurfacing unless cracked through
  • Roof replacement if 5+ years of life remain
  • Landscaping beyond mulch, mow, and edging
  • Bedroom paint colors that already read neutral
  • Anything cosmetic that a buyer will want to redo

Chapter 04 · Staging

What good staging looks like.

Staging is not decoration. It is the bridge between how you live in your house and how a buyer pictures themselves living there. Five rooms do almost all of the work.

pieces chosen for the room, not wheeled in from a warehouse

Rental staging is fine, and it works well for vacant homes. For lived-in homes, the best results usually come from pieces selected specifically for the room (sofas, rugs, art, lamps, books) chosen for the way the home photographs. I stage every one of my listings myself, with pieces I have collected over the years for exactly this work.

The goal is not "Pinterest perfect." The goal is a buyer pausing at the threshold of every room and picturing themselves there.

The consultation is on me. I walk every room and leave you with a written plan, no charge. Hands-on staging is charged separately: roughly $1,000 to $1,800 occupied, $2,500 and up vacant, depending on the size of the house, how much furniture we bring in, and the logistics. I keep it low on purpose, and unlike a staging company I do not charge a monthly furniture rental fee, which commonly runs $800 to $2,000 a month. Most sellers earn it back many times over.

The 5 rooms that decide it

  1. Living room. A single conversation grouping, one anchor rug, no more than three pieces of art on any wall. Buyers stand here longest.
  2. Kitchen. Counters cleared to two intentional objects. Cabinet contents reduced by half. Pendant bulbs matching, warm white.
  3. Primary bedroom. Bed made hotel-style, two nightstands matching, lamps on, blinds tilted up. Closets must look half-empty.
  4. Primary bath. Towels rolled, mirror perfect, two products visible total. No personal medication, no clutter on the back of the toilet.
  5. Front porch & entry. The first 8 seconds of every showing. Doormat, planter, paint touched up, working light, swept walk.
