the thing nobody tells you

Zillow will not
list your house.

Not in Washington. Not any more. And that is most of your buyers.

If you sold a house by owner a few years ago, you probably posted it on Zillow yourself. That door is closed here now.

Zillow is a member of our MLS. In Washington State that means Zillow does not take for-sale-by-owner listings. Other states still allow owners to post directly. Ours does not. If your house is not in the Northwest MLS, it does not appear on the site nearly every buyer in this county opens first.

I have watched people learn this on day two, after the sign is already in the yard and they have told everyone at work. It is not a small inconvenience. It is the difference between your house being seen and your house being invisible.

What that leaves you. A yard sign. Word of mouth. Facebook groups and Craigslist. Whatever foot traffic your street gets. That can be enough if you already have a buyer. It is rarely enough if you are trying to find one.

what you are actually signing

The paperwork is
the whole job.

This is the part people underestimate, and it is the part that follows you after closing.

Before it goes up

  • Form 17, the seller disclosure. Required on most Washington residential sales. What you knew and did not say is what comes back at you.
  • Title report, pulled early, so an old lien or easement is not a surprise in week six
  • Lead-based paint disclosure if the house was built before 1978
  • Anything your water, septic or well requires
  • What your HOA has to hand a buyer, and how long they take to produce it

Once you have an offer

  • The purchase and sale agreement, and every addendum attached to it
  • Earnest money, who holds it and what makes it refundable
  • Inspection response and repair negotiation, on a clock
  • Appraisal, and what happens if it comes in under contract price
  • Financing and title contingency dates, each with a deadline you cannot miss
  • Escrow instructions, the closing statement, and the keys

the real risk.

Almost nobody gets into trouble over the price. They get into trouble over a date they missed or a thing they did not disclose. Those are the two ways a sale falls apart or comes back months later, and both of them are paperwork.

the decision that costs the most

Whether you offer to pay
the buyer's agent.

It is optional. It is also the fastest way to make your house invisible in this market.

A buyer cannot finance their agent's commission. It is not part of the loan. So if you do not offer to pay it, that is roughly two and a half to three percent the buyer has to produce in cash, on top of the down payment, on top of closing costs.

On a $700,000 house that is somewhere around $17,500 to $21,000 in cash that has to appear from somewhere.

Now put that in this market. Mortgage rates crossed 7% in September 2026, the first time since January 2025. On the Snohomish County median with 20% down, the payment is already about $105 a month higher than it was two weeks earlier. Buyers are stretched and they know it.

Ask a buyer to find another twenty thousand dollars and they will not argue with you about it. They will just go see the house down the street that does offer it. And right now there are plenty of those houses.

What this does not mean. It is not a rule and nobody can tell you that you have to. It is a choice about how many buyers can physically afford to write on your house. Make it on purpose, with the number in front of you, rather than by leaving the field blank.

knowing when

When to stop
and hand it over.

Switching is not a failure. It is information, and it takes a few days.

  1. Two weeks with no showings. Not no offers. No showings. That means nobody is finding it or nobody likes what they see online, and both of those are fixable by someone else.
  2. Showings that go quiet. People come through and you never hear back. Usually price, sometimes presentation, occasionally something about the house nobody will say to your face.
  3. You have a deadline. A closing on the next place, a job, a lease, a family situation. Time pressure and a learning curve are a terrible combination.
  4. The offer arrives and you freeze. Inspection response, an appraisal gap, a financing contingency. This is the moment where an afternoon of bad judgment costs more than a full commission.
  5. It is affecting your household. I have watched this one land hardest. If it is making someone in the house miserable, that is a real cost even if it never shows up on the closing statement.

and about the house itself.

Your home is set up for living in. It is not set up for selling. Those are completely different things and it is not about your taste, it is about how a stranger moves through a room. One seller walked in after we staged hers and said: "I love the decor. It's crazy how it changes the whole dynamics of the house." That reaction is the whole argument, and it is hard to believe until you see it in your own rooms.
