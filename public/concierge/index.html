What's included

What concierge actually covers

Every concierge listing is scoped to the home. Not every category below is needed for every property — we walk through it together at the listing consultation and write a scope that matches your home's actual condition.

Deep clean

Whole-home professional clean before photos. Carpets when applicable. Windows inside and out where weather permits.

Paint + cosmetic repair

Touch-up or full repaint of high-impact rooms. Drywall patches, baseboard fixes, trim work. Scoped to what photos and showings need.

Light handyman

The list every seller knows: door handles, leaky faucets, light fixtures, smoke detectors, the squeaky hinge that's been there for years.

In-house staging

I'm a stager. Furniture rearrangement, accessory layering, and full furniture rental for vacant properties when the home calls for it.

Landscaping refresh

Lawn cut, beds edged, hedges trimmed, a few seasonal plantings at the entry. Curb-appeal-grade, not full re-landscape.

Vendor coordination

I manage the vendors, the schedule, and the access. You don't take time off work to wait for the painter. That's the whole point of "concierge."

Pre-listing professional photography

Pro photos and Matterport 3D walk-through are part of every Pelham Group listing — included whether you choose concierge or not.

Larger repairs (case-by-case)

Roof, HVAC, sewer scope follow-ups, larger structural work — handled through trusted vendor partners and only when the math says it'll come back at sale. Disclosed and approved before work starts.

How it works

How pay-at-closing actually works

This is the part I want you to read carefully — because it's the part that gets misunderstood when someone summarizes it in one sentence.

1

We meet for a listing consultation.

I walk the home. We talk about price, timing, condition, and what would move the home into a stronger listing position. No pitch, no pressure — same as every conversation I have.

2

I write a scoped concierge proposal.

It lists every line of work, the estimated cost, the vendor, and the timeline. You see all of it before anything happens. If a number changes during the work, I tell you before that scope expands — not after.

3

We sign the listing agreement and a concierge addendum.

The addendum names the scope, the total estimated fee, and the structure: those fees are paid from your proceeds at closing. If the home doesn't close, the addendum specifies what happens with the work performed. You see all of that before you sign.

4

I run the work.

Vendors come to your home on a schedule we agree on. I coordinate access, supervise the work, and quality-check before the next step. You're not running point on contractors — that's my job.

5

We list, market, and close.

Pro photos, Matterport, the full Pelham Group marketing push. At closing, the concierge fees come out of proceeds, just as the addendum laid out. You see them on the settlement statement. No surprise.

Qualifying conditions

Concierge is an option, not a default. It's a fit when the math works. Here's how I evaluate fit at the listing consultation:

Conditions for concierge eligibility

  • Home is owner-occupied or seller has clear authority to authorize work
  • Estimated proceeds at closing comfortably cover concierge fees plus standard closing costs
  • Property is located within the Pelham Group NW service area (Snohomish + bordering counties)
  • Listing timeline allows for the prep work to complete before list date
  • Seller signs the listing agreement and the concierge addendum before any work begins
  • Mortgage status, lien position, and existing liabilities are reviewed and disclosed
  • Scope is mutually agreed in writing — including the cap on total concierge spend

If something doesn't fit those conditions — say, the math is too tight, or the timeline doesn't work — I'll say so at the listing consultation and we'll talk about what would. Sometimes that means a smaller concierge scope. Sometimes it means standard listing service. Sometimes it means waiting six months. I tell you which one I think makes sense for your situation.

Questions I get

FAQ

Is concierge free?

No. Concierge fees are deferred — they come out of your proceeds at closing instead of being paid upfront. They are real costs, written into the addendum, and visible on the settlement statement. The benefit is the timing, not the price.

What happens if my home doesn't sell?

The concierge addendum specifies exactly what happens. Generally, if the listing terminates without a sale, the seller becomes responsible for the documented vendor costs incurred to date. We talk through this scenario at the listing consultation, and you see the language before you sign anything.

Is there a minimum or maximum spend?

There's no fixed minimum. Maximum is whatever the math supports for your specific home and proceeds — and we cap that number in the addendum so it can't grow without your written approval.

Who are your vendor partners?

I work with a small group of vetted Snohomish County contractors and one in-network repair company my husband and I have a business relationship with. Any vendor partnership where I have a financial relationship is disclosed in writing in your concierge addendum before any work begins. You always have the right to use a vendor of your own choosing instead.

Do all of your listings use concierge?

No. Most listings don't need it. Some homes are already in great shape — pro photos and staging are enough. I'll tell you which one I think your home is at the listing consultation. I'm not paid more if you choose concierge versus standard listing service, so my advice on this is honest.

How is this different from a "fix and list" company that flips the work for a percentage?

I'm a Realtor with an in-house staging practice and a coordinated vendor network — not an investor buying a stake in your home. You stay in title. There's no equity transfer. The fees are documented vendor costs plus a concierge coordination component, all itemized in the addendum.

How fast can you start?

From listing consultation to crews on site is usually 7 to 14 days for standard scopes. Larger scopes take longer because of permitting and vendor scheduling. We map the timeline at consultation.

Do you do this for buyers too?

Concierge is a listing-side option. For buyers, I work with a network of trusted contractors and lenders if you need referrals — but the concierge structure is specific to sellers because it depends on closing proceeds.
