4. Voice Characteristics

How Kim communicates. Every piece of content should reflect these qualities.

W

Warm + Authoritative

Equal parts empathy and data. She feels what her clients feel, AND she backs it up with numbers. Never one without the other.

C

Conversational but Professional

Like a trusted advisor over coffee, not a sales pitch. Friendly and accessible, yet always credible and knowledgeable.

D

Data-Backed

Always includes specific numbers, stats, and details. Never vague claims. "104.4% sale-to-list ratio" not "great results."

E

Empathetic

She understands the emotional weight of selling a family home, buying a first home, downsizing after decades. This isn't clinical — it's human.

C

Community-Rooted

She lives here, boats here, walks her dog here. This isn't just her market — it's her home. That authenticity matters.

N

Never Salesy

She presents facts and lets clients decide. No pressure. No urgency tactics. No manipulation. Just honest, helpful guidance.

5. Language Patterns

DO Use

  • "We" and "your" — collaborative language
  • Specific numbers: "104.4% sale-to-list ratio" not "great results"
  • Neighborhood names and local references
  • Personal stories that connect to expertise
  • "Walking alongside" / "Your next chapter" / "What home means"
  • Questions that invite reflection
  • Warm, welcoming tone

✗ DON'T Use

  • "Buy now!" or urgency pressure tactics
  • Generic superlatives: "best agent ever" / "amazing results"
  • Corporate jargon or marketing speak
  • Exclamation marks in headlines
  • Emojis in professional content
  • Fear-based messaging ("Don't miss out!")
  • Clichés ("Your dream home awaits")

Tone in Practice

❌ Don't Say This
"Act now! Our incredible homes are selling FAST! Don't miss out on your dream home. Kim Pelham has the best results in the area!"
Say This Instead
"The Everett market is moving. Homes listed with Kim's strategy sell at 104.4% of list price on average — well above the county average of 98.61%. That's the difference between a prepared strategy and hoping for the best. Let's talk about your next chapter."

8. Content Formats & Guidelines

Blog Articles

  • Length: 1,200–2,000 words
  • Opening: Hook that connects personal to professional. Why this matters.
  • Body: Data-rich with styled stat cards, real examples, actionable advice
  • Structure: FAQ section (5 questions) for SEO/AEO/GEO benefit
  • Closing: Warm CTA. ("Let's talk about your situation," not "Contact us today!")
  • Technical: Include schema markup (Article, FAQPage, LocalBusiness)

Email Newsletters

  • Opening: Personal moment (life, community, season). Then transition to market.
  • Body: Market data snapshot (3 key stats). Featured article teaser.
  • Section: "Kim's Corner" — editorial opinion on the market or a client success
  • Closing: Warm, inviting CTA. Invite questions and conversation.

Social Media

  • Facebook: Longer form, community-focused. Questions that drive engagement. Market insights. Local events.
  • Instagram: Visual-first. Carousel education. Neighborhood tours. Reel concepts (market tips, client stories, seasonal updates). Consistent hashtag strategy.
  • LinkedIn: Thought leadership. Professional authority. Line breaks for algorithm. Industry insights, published articles, speaking engagements.
  • YouTube: Educational content. Market updates. Neighborhood deep-dives. Senior downsizing guides. Client testimonials (with permission).
